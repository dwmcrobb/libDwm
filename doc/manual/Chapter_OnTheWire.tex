\section{Encoded integral classes}

\subsection{\texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64} encoding}\label{encoding:DwmEncodedU64}
\hyperref[class:EncodedU64]{\texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64}} is encoded with
an encoding byte followed by one to eight bytes representing an
unsigned 64-bit integer value.

\EncodedEightByteUnsignedOverview{{},{},{},{},{},{},{},{}}{value (\texttt{size}+1 bytes)}{$byte_0$}{$byte_{size}$}{Dwm::EncodedU64 encoding}

\subsubsection{Encoding byte}

% \EncodedEightByteUnsignedExample{{},{},{},{},{},{},{},{}}{}{Encoding byte}

Four of the eight bits of the encoding byte are utilized.
Table~\ref{table:EncodedU64EndianBit} shows the meaning of the
\texttt{endian} bit.

\begin{table}[H]
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
  \textbf{endian} & \textbf{description} \\ \hline
  0 & \texttt{value} is in little-endian format \\ \hline
  1 & \texttt{value} is in big-endian format \\
\end{tabular}
\caption{Meaning of endian bit in \texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64} encoding byte}
\label{table:EncodedU64EndianBit}
\end{center}
\end{table}

The \texttt{size} bits indicate the length of the value (in bytes)
following the encoding byte, minus 1:

  $length\ of\ \texttt{value} = \texttt{size} + 1$

Table~\ref{table:EncodedU64SizeBits} shows the translation from
\texttt{size} to/from the length of the value.

\begin{table}[H]
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{r|l|l|l|l|l|l|l|l}
  \textbf{\texttt{size}} & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\
  \textbf{length of value (bytes)} & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\caption{meaning of size bits in \texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64} encoding byte}
\label{table:EncodedU64SizeBits}
\end{table}

The remaining bits in the encoding byte are unused.

The \texttt{value} of a
\hyperref[class:EncodedU64]{\texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64}} is always
written in native byte order (typically little-endian).  The read
member functions of
\hyperref[class:EncodedU64]{\texttt{Dwm::EncodedU64}} will convert
endianness if needed.
